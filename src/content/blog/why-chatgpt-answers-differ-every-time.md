# Why ChatGPT gives a different answer every time you ask

ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude write answers one word at a time, choosing each word from a set of likely options, so the same question can give a different answer each time. Search tools and live web results add more variation. In one study, ChatGPT and Google's AI returned the same list of brands less than 1% of the time across repeated runs. So one answer proves very little about your business.

We teach this idea in our course, so we're not neutral. The numbers below come from third-party studies, and we say where they have limits.

## How does an assistant produce an answer?

A language model estimates how likely each possible next word is, picks one, and repeats until the answer is complete. The design behind this, called the transformer, was published in 2017, in the paper [Attention Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762). Because the pick is made from probabilities, not read off a fixed list, two runs can diverge early and end up in different places.

On top of that, assistants that browse the web rewrite your question into one or more searches. OpenAI says ChatGPT search [rewrites queries and sometimes uses other search providers](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt). Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use [query fan-out](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), meaning several related searches. Different searches can pull different pages.

## What did studies measure?

- **SparkToro and Gumshoe, late 2025.** Using 600 volunteers, they ran 12 prompts through ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI a combined 2,961 times. The chance that ChatGPT or Google's AI gave the same brand list twice was under 1 in 100, and the same list in the same order about 1 in 1,000. Their [write-up](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/) says appearance across many runs was a steadier measure than position. Gumshoe sells AI tracking tools, and the study isn't peer reviewed.
- **BrightLocal, 2026.** For local searches, a business reappeared in later ChatGPT runs about [50% of the time](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/). US locations, from a local SEO software vendor.

There is a hopeful side. Brands strongly tied to a topic keep showing up. In the SparkToro data, a few headphone brands appeared in 55% to 77% of responses.

## What should you do about it?

1. **Never judge from one answer.** Run each question several times and record the date.
2. **Measure how often you appear,** not where. Position in one list is noisy.
3. **Check more than one assistant.** Their sources overlap only partly.
4. **Track over months.** A rising appearance rate is a hint, not proof.

## Questions, answered

### Why does ChatGPT give different answers to the same question?
It picks each word from probabilities, and web-connected versions also rewrite your question into searches. Both add variation. Studies show brand lists rarely repeat exactly.

### Is a different answer a sign that ChatGPT is wrong?
Not necessarily. Several different answers can all be reasonable. OpenAI also warns that search results and citations [can be incomplete, outdated or incorrect](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt), so check important facts.

### How many times should I ask the same question?
SparkToro's researchers suggest asking many times, at least 60 to 100, to learn an assistant's usual set of brands. For a small business, we suggest 3 runs per question to start, which is our rule of thumb and not a researched number.

### Can I make ChatGPT give the same answer?
Not reliably in the app. Developers can lower randomness in API settings, but that doesn't show what your customers see.
