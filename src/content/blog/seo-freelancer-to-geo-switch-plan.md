# From SEO freelancer to GEO: a practical 90-day switch plan

You don't have to start over. Google says optimizing for its generative AI features is still SEO, so crawlability, quality content and authority carry over. What's new is checking what assistants actually say, building mentions on other sites and measuring appearance rate instead of position. A realistic switch is a 90-day plan: learn in month 1, audit a real site in month 2 and make your first offers in month 3.

We teach GEO, so we want you to try it. We can't say how many freelancers earn from it, and we found no data.

## What carries over from SEO?

Google's [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says optimizing for generative AI search is "still SEO" for its features, and recommends unique expert content, crawlable and well-structured pages, page experience and Search Console monitoring. Its [AI features page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. Your SEO skills already cover that.

## How much do rankings and AI citations overlap?

Studies disagree, so don't promise either way:

- Ahrefs found [76% of AI Overview citations came from the top 10 in July 2025, and about 38% in March 2026](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/) with a changed method.
- Conductor reported that [57% of AI Overview citations came from outside the top 10](https://www.conductor.com/academy/aio-vs-organic-rankings/) in a US study from June to August 2026.
- For ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot, Ahrefs found only [about 12% of cited links were in Google's top 10](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-overlap/).

All three are vendors. The lesson for you: ranking helps and isn't enough, so check each assistant directly. See [why rank doesn't equal mentions](/blog/google-rank-vs-chatgpt-mentions).

## The 90-day plan

**Days 1 to 30: learn and set up.** Take a short course such as [The GEO Blueprint](/#curriculum) and read Google's two AI pages. Set up GA4 and Search Console on your own site. Run the prompt panel for your own business. Follow our [30-day plan](/blog/learn-geo-30-days) if you want weekly tasks.

**Days 31 to 60: audit real sites.** Offer to audit one or two existing SEO clients, with permission, as a trial. Use [the audit steps](/blog/how-to-run-a-geo-audit). Write findings in a one-page note.

**Days 61 to 90: package and offer.** Turn your best audit into a sample report. Build the three packages in [GEO service packages](/blog/geo-service-packages-sample-tiers), and make offers to 5 to 10 existing clients.

This is our suggestion, not a forecast. You might win clients, or none.

## What to avoid

- **Promising mentions or timelines.** OpenAI says [placement isn't guaranteed](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt).
- **Dropping SEO.** AI chatbots sent [0.28% of web traffic](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/) in Ahrefs' March 2026 tracker, so search still matters.
- **Rebranding without learning.** Clients can tell. Do an audit before you add GEO to your service page.

## Questions, answered

### Is GEO replacing SEO?
No evidence says so. Google calls optimizing for its AI features still SEO, and Google remains a far larger source of traffic than AI chatbots.

### Can I add GEO to my SEO service now?
Yes, after you've done at least one real audit. Position it as an extension and say what you check.

### What should I charge?
Price from your hours and scope. See [how much to charge for a GEO audit](/blog/how-much-geo-audit).

### Do I need new tools?
Not to start. A spreadsheet, GA4 and Search Console cover the first audits.
