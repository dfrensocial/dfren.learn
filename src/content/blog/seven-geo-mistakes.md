# Seven GEO mistakes that waste money (and what to do instead)

The seven most common ways to waste money on GEO: blocking AI crawlers by accident, stuffing keywords for AI, relying on llms.txt, judging by one AI answer, scoring your site by asking a chatbot, promising rankings to clients and starting without a baseline. Each is avoidable, and each has a source or study behind it below.

We teach GEO, so we've seen these in practice. Where a point is our experience and not a study, we say so.

## 1. Blocking the crawlers by accident

If a bot can't fetch your pages, an assistant can't name you from them. OpenAI says to be eligible for ChatGPT search you should allow [OAI-SearchBot and confirm your host or CDN allows its IP addresses](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt). Firewalls and security plugins often block bots by default. **Instead:** check robots.txt and your firewall settings. Allowing a search bot is separate from allowing a training bot, and [OpenAI documents the difference](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).

## 2. Keyword stuffing for AI

The [original GEO paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.09735) tested 9 methods and found keyword stuffing did little or nothing, while adding sources, quotations and statistics helped. **Instead:** add real facts, numbers and sources.

## 3. Treating llms.txt as a strategy

Google's guide says Google Search [ignores llms.txt and Markdown files](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), and a 300,000-domain study found [no measurable link with AI citations](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/llms-txt-shows-no-clear-effect-on-ai-citations-based-on-300k-domains/561542/). It's harmless, not a plan. **Instead:** fix pages first. See [is llms.txt enough?](/blog/llms-txt-vs-geo-is-it-enough-2027)

## 4. Judging by one answer

AI answers vary run to run. SparkToro and Gumshoe found the same brand list came back [less than 1 in 100 times](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/). **Instead:** run prompts several times, track appearance rate and use it as a free quick check, not as proof. See [the free method](/blog/know-if-ai-recommends-your-business).

## 5. Scoring your site by asking a chatbot

A chatbot has no fixed rubric, so scores differ between runs, and several tools work by wrapping your URL in a prompt. That's our explanation, and you can test it by asking three times. **Instead:** use a written checklist or a rule-based tool. See [why the score changes](/blog/why-chatgpt-gives-different-website-score).

## 6. Promising rankings or mentions

OpenAI says [placement in ChatGPT search is not guaranteed](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt). India's [CCPA guidelines](https://ccpa.doca.gov.in/files/CCPA_Notification.pdf) require claims about facts to be capable of substantiation. **Instead:** promise your work and your reporting. See [a proposal without promises](/blog/geo-proposal-without-promising-rankings).

## 7. Starting without a baseline

If you don't record today's state, you can't show change later. This one is our practice, not a study. **Instead:** set up analytics and Search Console, run a dated prompt panel and save it. See [what to track first](/blog/what-to-track-before-geo-client).

## Bonus: flooding the site with thin AI content

Our course lists this as a common mistake: publishing low-effort pages generated by AI with no real depth or examples. Google's guide asks for [unique expert or experienced takes](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). **Instead:** publish fewer pages with real facts, sources and your own experience.

## A quick self-check

| Question | If "no", fix this |
|---|---|
| Can AI bots reach my pages? | Mistake 1 |
| Do my pages carry real facts and sources? | Mistakes 2 and bonus |
| Am I tracking over time, with a baseline? | Mistakes 4 and 7 |
| Is my scoring method repeatable? | Mistake 5 |
| Are my promises realistic? | Mistake 6 |

## Questions, answered

### What is the biggest GEO mistake?
Blocking crawlers or leaving pages unindexed, because nothing else works if assistants can't read your pages.

### Does keyword stuffing work for AI?
In the original research it did little or nothing. Facts, quotations and sources did better.

### Do I need llms.txt?
No. Google Search ignores it, and we found no evidence it helps citations.

### How do I avoid wasting money on GEO tools?
Start with free tools and a spreadsheet, and add a paid tool only when you have enough prompts or clients. See [tools by budget](/blog/ai-visibility-tools-by-budget).
