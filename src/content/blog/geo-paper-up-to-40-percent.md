# The GEO research paper: what "up to 40%" really means

The paper that named GEO reports that adding statistics, quotations and cited sources to a page raised its visibility in AI answers by about 30 to 40% in the authors' own test. That's the "up to 40%" you see quoted. It's a lab result on 2 AI systems, not a promise for a real business. Keyword stuffing did no better than doing nothing.

We teach GEO and sell a course on it, so we have a stake. We read the full text, on 3 November 2026, and report the numbers as the paper gives them.

## Who wrote it and when

"GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" is by Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan and Ameet Deshpande. It was first posted on arXiv on 16 November 2023 and last revised on 28 June 2024. Its abstract says GEO [can boost visibility by up to 40%](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) in generative engine responses. We read the [full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.09735) for the method.

## How they tested it

- **A benchmark of 10,000 queries.** The authors built GEO-bench from 9 sources. It covers 25 domains, such as arts, health and games. About 80% of the queries are informational.
- **A simple AI engine.** For each query, they fetched the top 5 Google results and had gpt-3.5-turbo write an answer based on them.
- **Two visibility measures.** One counts how much of the answer comes from your source, weighted by position. The other is a score from an AI judge on 7 qualities, such as relevance and likelihood of a click.
- **9 page changes.** They rewrote a source page in 9 ways and measured how its visibility changed.

## What worked, and what didn't

| Change tested | Result in the paper |
|---|---|
| Cite sources | About 30 to 40% gain on the position-weighted measure |
| Add quotations | About 30 to 40% gain |
| Add statistics | About 30 to 40% gain |
| Easy to understand | About 15 to 30% gain |
| Fluency optimisation | About 15 to 30% gain |
| Authoritative tone | No significant improvement |
| Keyword stuffing | Little or no gain, slightly below baseline (17.8 vs 19.5) |
| Unique words | Did not perform |

On the AI-judged measure, the top 3 gained about 15 to 30%.

Two more findings stand out. Combining fluency optimisation with statistics beat any single change by more than 5.5% on a 200-example subset. And citing sources lifted pages ranked 5th in Google by 115.1% on average, while the top-ranked page lost 30.3%.

## What happened on Perplexity

The authors also tested on Perplexity.ai, a live system. Adding quotations improved the position-weighted measure by 22%. Adding statistics improved the judged measure by up to 37%. Keyword stuffing did about 10% worse than the baseline.

## What the "up to 40%" leaves out

- **It's an upper bound.** The abstract says "up to 40%". The paper's own Table 1 caption cites 41% and 28% for the best methods on the two measures, so even the headline varies slightly.
- **Only 2 AI systems.** The authors say methods may need to adapt as these systems change.
- **Rankings weren't tested.** They didn't evaluate how Google ranking itself interacts, because it's a black box.
- **Labels can be noisy.** Query tags were made automatically.
- **It's a benchmark, not a business.** No real client's sales or traffic were measured.
- **It's from 2023 and 2024.** AI systems have changed since. We found no independent study that repeats it on real businesses.

## What to do with it

Use it as a checklist, not a promise. On a page you want AI to quote:

1. **Add statistics** where you have them, with the source and date.
2. **Add short quotations** from named, credible people, if they're real.
3. **Cite your sources** in the text, with links.
4. **Write clearly.** Plain, fluent writing also gained.
5. **Don't stuff keywords.** It didn't help.

These are cheap changes that also make a page more useful to people. That's a better reason to make them than a promised percentage.

## An illustrative example

This is an illustrative example, not a real client. A Chennai dental clinic's page says "We offer the best root canal treatment." That sentence gives an AI nothing it can quote. A stronger page would give one real figure from the clinic's own records, with the date, and name a public health source for any medical fact. The point isn't the exact words. It's replacing a claim with a specific, sourced fact.

## Questions, answered

### What does "up to 40%" mean in the GEO paper?
It means the best page changes raised visibility by about 30 to 40% on one measure in the authors' own test. It's an upper bound from a benchmark of 10,000 queries on 2 AI systems. It doesn't mean a real business will gain 40%.

### Which GEO methods worked best in the paper?
Citing sources, adding quotations and adding statistics, with about 30 to 40% gains on the position-weighted measure. Easy-to-understand and fluent writing gained about 15 to 30%. Authoritative tone and keyword stuffing didn't help.

### Does keyword stuffing work for GEO?
No. In the paper it gave little or no gain and scored slightly below baseline. On Perplexity it did about 10% worse than baseline. Write clearly and add facts instead.

### Has anyone repeated the GEO study?
We found no independent replication on real businesses in our search. The authors themselves list limits: 2 AI systems, noisy labels and changing queries. Treat the results as a useful starting point.
