# How to sell GEO services to your existing SEO clients (without overpromising)

Start with a small, low-risk offer: a baseline check of what AI assistants say about the client today, plus a short list of fixes. Show them evidence from public sources, not promises. Then offer GEO as an add-on to the SEO work you already do. Google says optimizing for its AI features is still SEO, so you're extending your service, not replacing it.

We teach GEO and sell a course, so we want you to try this. We also want you to avoid claims that can't be backed up.

## Why clients are open to the conversation

You can point to public numbers, with their limits:

- **Usage.** India has about [100 million weekly ChatGPT users](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/02/15/india-has-100m-weekly-active-chatgpt-users-sam-altman-says/), per a February 2026 report. Google's AI Overviews had [2 billion monthly users](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/23/googles-ai-overviews-have-2b-monthly-users-ai-mode-100m-in-the-us-and-india) by July 2025.
- **Behaviour.** Pew Research found people clicked a result in [8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, against 15% without](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appeared-in-the-results/). That's US data.
- **Honesty about size.** Ahrefs' tracker found AI chatbots sent [0.28% of web traffic](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/) in March 2026. Say so. A client who hears the real number trusts you more.

## How do you position it?

Say: "Your SEO work already covers the base. Customers now also ask assistants. Let's check what they say about you." That's accurate, and Google's [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says optimizing for generative AI search is still SEO. Don't say SEO is dead.

## What do you offer first?

1. **A baseline check (small, fixed price).** Run 10 to 20 customer questions across assistants and log who is named, three runs each. See our [free method](/blog/know-if-ai-recommends-your-business).
2. **A short findings note.** What assistants say, what's missing on the client's pages and three fixes. The audit steps are in [how to run a GEO audit](/blog/how-to-run-a-geo-audit).
3. **An add-on, only if they want it.** A fix pass, then ongoing content. Sample packages are in [GEO service packages](/blog/geo-service-packages-sample-tiers).

Quote the baseline as a one-off, so the client can say yes without a long commitment. Prices are in [how much to charge for a GEO audit](/blog/how-much-geo-audit).

## What should you never promise?

India's [CCPA misleading-advertisement guidelines](https://ccpa.doca.gov.in/files/CCPA_Notification.pdf) require claims about facts to be capable of substantiation, and the [ASCI Code](https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code) says claims must be truthful. Neither names GEO, so the general test applies. In practice:

- **No guaranteed mentions or rankings.** OpenAI says [placement in ChatGPT search is not guaranteed](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt).
- **No fixed timeline.** Say weeks to a few months and agree to review at about three months.
- **No fake before-and-after.** Show real baselines with dates.

Put what you will and won't promise in the proposal. See [a GEO proposal that doesn't promise rankings](/blog/geo-proposal-without-promising-rankings).

## A simple pitch outline

1. The change: customers ask assistants, with two or three public numbers.
2. What we found for you: your baseline, in a one-page table.
3. What we'd fix first, in priority order.
4. How we'd measure: appearance rate monthly, with a review at three months.
5. What we can't promise.
6. Fee and next step.

## Questions, answered

### How do I explain GEO to an SEO client?
Say customers now ask AI assistants for recommendations, and you'll check what the assistants say about the client and fix gaps. Keep it to the baseline and three fixes. More in [how to explain GEO to a client](/blog/explain-geo-to-a-client).

### Should I charge extra for GEO?
It's a different deliverable, so a separate line is clearer, but quoting it as part of a retainer is fine too. Show what's included.

### What if the client asks for guaranteed results?
Decline politely and explain why: assistants vary, and placement isn't guaranteed. Offer measured progress against a baseline instead.

### Do I need a tool?
No. A spreadsheet works to start. See [GEO on a zero budget](/blog/geo-small-business-zero-budget).
