# How to write a GEO proposal that doesn't promise rankings

A good GEO proposal states what you'll check and change, how you'll measure progress and when you'll review, and says plainly what you can't promise. Skip guaranteed mentions, rankings and timelines. Nobody controls what an AI assistant says, and OpenAI itself says placement in ChatGPT search is not guaranteed. Clients trust a clear scope more than a big promise.

We teach this in our course, so we have a stake in the workflow. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

## Why avoid promises?

- **They can't be kept.** OpenAI says [ChatGPT ranks results using multiple factors and placement is not guaranteed](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt). Answers also vary run to run: ChatGPT and Google's AI gave the same brand list [less than 1% of the time](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/) in one study.
- **They can break advertising rules.** India's [CCPA guidelines](https://ccpa.doca.gov.in/files/CCPA_Notification.pdf) require claims about facts to be capable of substantiation and bar exaggerating a service's capability. The [ASCI Code](https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code) says ads must be truthful. They don't name GEO, so the general test applies. A proposal is a sales document, so write it as if a regulator might read it.
- **They create disputes.** If you promise "page 1 in ChatGPT in 60 days" and it doesn't happen, you own the problem.

## The structure

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| 1. Summary | The client's situation in three sentences, using their baseline |
| 2. Findings | A table of what assistants say and what's missing on their pages |
| 3. Scope | Exactly what you'll do, listed as deliverables |
| 4. Method | The checks and changes, in plain words |
| 5. Measurement | How you'll track progress and when you'll report |
| 6. Timeline | Delivery dates for your work, and a review point |
| 7. What we don't promise | Short, plain list |
| 8. Client responsibilities | Access, approvals, source facts |
| 9. Fees and terms | Price, payment, change process |

## Wording examples

Replace promise language with process language.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "We will get you recommended by ChatGPT" | "We will check what ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity say about your business and fix the gaps we find on your site" |
| "Results in 60 days" | "We expect changes to take weeks to a few months to appear, and we'll review against your baseline at about three months" |
| "Number 1 in AI answers" | "We'll report how often your business is named across a fixed set of customer questions, month by month" |
| "Guaranteed traffic from AI" | "We'll track visits from AI assistants in your analytics. These are currently a small share of web traffic" |

The last row is backed by data: Ahrefs' tracker found AI chatbots sent [0.28% of web traffic](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/) in March 2026.

## What to measure

Use appearance rate, not position, across a fixed prompt list run several times. Pair it with Search Console data, including Google's [Generative AI performance reports](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports), and referral visits in analytics. Take the baseline before work starts. See [what to track before you start](/blog/what-to-track-before-geo-client).

## The "what we don't promise" section

Short and specific, for example:

- We don't guarantee that any assistant will name your business.
- We don't control or know how assistants choose answers.
- Answers vary between runs and change as products change.
- We don't promise a particular timeline, ranking or traffic number.
- Results depend on your market, competitors and site, and on your team approving changes.

## A caution on testimonials and case studies

When you later use results in marketing, show real baselines, dates and the method. CCPA's guidelines also cover endorsements: they must reflect the endorser's genuine opinion, and any connection that might affect credibility must be disclosed.

## Questions, answered

### Can I promise a client rankings in AI answers?
We advise against it. No company publishes a ranking formula, and OpenAI says placement isn't guaranteed. Promise your work and your reporting instead.

### What should a GEO proposal include?
A baseline, findings, a defined scope, how progress is measured, a review point, what you don't promise, client responsibilities and fees.

### How long should I say GEO takes?
There's no published timeline. We tell clients to expect weeks to a few months and to review at about three months. See [how long GEO takes](/blog/how-long-does-geo-take).

### Do I need a lawyer to review it?
For standard terms and any regulated sector, yes. This article is general guidance.
