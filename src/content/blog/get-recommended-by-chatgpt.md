# How to get your business recommended by ChatGPT: 7 steps that are backed by evidence

You can't buy or force a recommendation from ChatGPT. You can make your business easier to find and easier to quote. The steps with the clearest support: let OpenAI's search crawler in, answer customer questions plainly on your pages, add sources and numbers, get listed and reviewed elsewhere, state your location clearly, keep pages fresh and check the results over time. OpenAI itself says [placement is not guaranteed](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt).

We sell a GEO course, so take our enthusiasm with some salt. For each step, we say how strong the evidence is.

## Step 1: Let OAI-SearchBot in (strong, from OpenAI)

OpenAI says [OAI-SearchBot](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) is the crawler that surfaces websites in ChatGPT's search features. GPTBot is a different bot used for training. You can allow one and block the other, and each is set separately in robots.txt.

To be eligible, OpenAI's help page says to allow OAI-SearchBot and [confirm your host or CDN allows OpenAI's published IP addresses](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt). That second part matters if your site sits behind a firewall such as Cloudflare. OpenAI says a robots.txt change can take [about 24 hours](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) to take effect.

## Step 2: Answer the question in the first two lines (moderate)

Put the direct answer at the top of each page, then the detail. Our reason is simple: an assistant that searches for pieces of an answer picks the page that gives one quickly. There's no OpenAI statement saying this, so call it good practice, not a rule.

## Step 3: Add sources, quotes and numbers (moderate, from research)

The [original GEO research paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.09735) tested 9 changes on 10,000 queries. Adding sources, quotations and statistics lifted visibility by roughly 30 to 40% in the authors' own test, while keyword stuffing did no better than doing nothing. The test used a research setup with a smaller model, not live ChatGPT. See [what the paper found](/blog/geo-paper-up-to-40-percent).

## Step 4: Be mentioned and reviewed elsewhere (moderate, correlation)

An [SE Ranking study](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/new-data-top-factors-influencing-chatgpt-citations/561954/) found sites listed on several review platforms averaged more ChatGPT citations than sites that weren't (4.6 to 6.3 against 1.8). It's a correlation across large sites. For local businesses, a [BrightLocal study of 800 ChatGPT searches](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/uncovering-chatgpt-search-sources/) in the US found business websites made up 58% of sources, business mentions 27% and directories 15%. The data is from November 2024, so check it again.

Practical version: claim your Google Business Profile, get listed in the directories your customers use and ask happy customers for honest reviews.

## Step 5: Say where you are (moderate, from OpenAI)

OpenAI says ChatGPT uses your approximate location for local results and suggests including a [city, neighbourhood or postal code](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt) in a question. Your pages should do the same. Write "Anna Nagar, Chennai" on the page, not only "near you". That's our suggestion, based on how people are told to ask.

## Step 6: Keep key pages fresh (weak to moderate)

In the SE Ranking data, pages updated within three months averaged 6 ChatGPT citations, against 3.6 for outdated ones. Again, a correlation. A quarterly refresh of your top pages with new facts is cheap, so it's worth doing either way.

## Step 7: Check, then check again (strong)

ChatGPT's answers change. SparkToro and Gumshoe found ChatGPT and Google's AI [returned the same list of brands less than 1% of the time](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-recommendations-change-with-nearly-every-query-sparktoro/566242) across 2,961 runs. So run the same 10 questions several times, note the date and look for a pattern. Here is [a free way to do it](/blog/know-if-ai-recommends-your-business).

## What won't work

- **Paying for a guaranteed mention.** We found no legitimate way to do this.
- **Stuffing pages with AI-bait phrases.** The research paper found keyword stuffing did no better than doing nothing.
- **Blocking the search bot and expecting a mention.** OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will [not be shown in ChatGPT search answers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots), though they may still appear as navigational links.

## Questions, answered

### Does ChatGPT recommend businesses for free?
ChatGPT's search answers aren't sold as placements, as far as we found. OpenAI says results are ranked using multiple factors and placement isn't guaranteed. Ads in ChatGPT, if you see them, are a separate product we haven't studied here.

### How long does it take to appear in ChatGPT?
We don't know, and nobody has published a reliable timeline. OpenAI says a crawler setting change can take about 24 hours. Being crawled doesn't mean being recommended, so plan in weeks to months, not days.

### Do I need a paid tool to get recommended?
No tool is needed to do the steps above. Tools can help you track results, but a spreadsheet and a set of repeated questions works to begin with.

### Does ChatGPT understand Tamil questions about my business?
We haven't found an official statement on how well ChatGPT handles local Tamil queries. Test it yourself by asking the same question in Tamil and in English, and write your pages in the language your customers use.
